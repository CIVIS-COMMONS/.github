# CIVIS Commons Governance

**Organization:** ORIGO CIVIS · CIVIS Commons  
**Status:** Foundational governance proposal

## 1. Purpose

CIVIS Commons exists to support open-source software, independent research, technical documentation, and responsible technology evaluation.

Its governance should protect the integrity of its work while respecting the independence of contributors and projects.

## 2. Founding Principles

CIVIS Commons is guided by five commitments:

- **Openness:** Make methods, decisions, and project documentation accessible whenever practical.
- **Independence:** Respect the autonomy of individual projects and their maintainers.
- **Evidence:** Ground technical claims in reproducible observations and clearly state limitations.
- **Stewardship:** Exercise authority carefully, transparently, and only where responsibility has been accepted.
- **Continuity:** Preserve relevant intent, context, and history so future contributors can understand past decisions.

## 3. Decision-Making

Organizational decisions should be documented in proportion to their significance.

Decisions affecting project direction, licensing, governance, or community participation should include their rationale and relevant context.

Where practical, affected contributors should have an opportunity to provide feedback before significant changes are finalized.

Urgent actions necessary to protect systems or users may be taken promptly, with an explanation recorded afterward.

## 4. Project Independence

Each project retains its own scope, maintainership arrangements, license, and contribution rules.

Participation in CIVIS Commons does not automatically grant authority over a project's repository, roadmap, intellectual property, or release process.

Any transfer of repository ownership or delegation of administrative authority must be explicit.

## 5. Contributions and Attribution

Contributions should be evaluated according to documented project requirements and relevant technical or community considerations.

Contributors should receive appropriate attribution. Project licenses and contribution agreements, where applicable, govern the rights associated with contributed work.
